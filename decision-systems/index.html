Decision systemsUpdated July 2026

What is a decision system?

A decision system is the set of roles, authorities, information flows, and feedback loops that determines who decides what—and how the organization learns whether a decision worked.

Most groups start with a choice of voting tool or meeting format. The harder design question comes first: which decisions require broad consent, which require informed judgment, and which should be reversible experiments? Anode maps those decision types before choosing the mechanism.

Design questions we answer

  • Which decisions are reversible, and which create durable commitments?
  • Who has the context, expertise, or lived stake needed to make each call?
  • What should be delegated, escalated, sampled, or decided by consent?
  • How can people challenge a decision without making every decision collective?
  • What evidence will tell the group to keep, revise, or reverse the mechanism?

Typical outputs

Engagements can produce a decision-rights map, delegation and escalation rules, role charters, proposal and review workflows, voting or consent criteria, and a measurement plan for decision quality. The output is designed to be run by the team—not admired as a diagram.
