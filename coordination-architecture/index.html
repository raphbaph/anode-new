Coordination architectureUpdated July 2026

What is coordination architecture?

Coordination architecture is the structure that lets interdependent people act together: who belongs to which unit, what each unit can decide, how work crosses boundaries, and how the whole system sees what is happening.

Growth often creates a false choice between central control and complete autonomy. A better design gives small, trusted units clear mandates and usable interfaces. People close to the work can adapt locally; the wider network can still align around shared constraints, standards, and signals.

Design questions we answer

  • What is the smallest useful unit of trust, work, and accountability?
  • Which interfaces need shared protocols, service levels, or common language?
  • Where should information aggregate—and where should it stay close to the edge?
  • How do units resolve conflicts without waiting for a central authority?
  • What feedback tells the system when to split, merge, or change a mandate?

Typical outputs

Our work can produce network maps, unit and role definitions, mandate and interface specifications, coordination rituals, escalation paths, and signals dashboards. We test the architecture against realistic handoffs, delays, conflicts, and partial information.
