WHAT THIS MODEL ANSWERS

Make the trade-offs explicit.

Compare revenue distribution and staking incentives under a common set of balances and assumptions. Direct balances mean all supply outside the programme’s fintech balance wallets; this residual is not a count of retail users.

Current 90/10 rule

Staker revenue = distributable yield × (direct balances + 10% × fintech balances).

Fixed take rate

Staker revenue = distributable yield × total supply × fixed take rate. The rest is attributed to the aggregate fintech pool in this comparison. Implementation must specify eligibility and allocation between fintechs.

OC-responsive illustration

The staker allocation on fintech revenue increases linearly as observed OC falls from the trigger to the target, then remains capped. Direct-balance revenue continues to accrue to stakers. This version has no trailing average, delay, hysteresis or asymmetric recovery. It is a discussion sketch, not a deployable policy.

ASSUMPTIONS & LIMITS

Revenue and risk are different dimensions.

OC = dollar value of staked RSR ÷ eUSD supply. This is a market-value backstop ratio, not guaranteed recoverable protection in a collateral default. Staking APR = annual staker revenue ÷ dollar value of staked RSR.

Distributable yield is an input after any excluded fees, collateral costs and revenue sinks. The model assumes equal yield attribution per eUSD and $1 per eUSD. Fintech revenue is before saver rewards, incentives and operating costs; it is not profit.

RSR stake, price and required return are independent scenario assumptions. No price appreciation, new staking, compounding or slashing is predicted. Required APR is a user assumption, not a finding that 6% adequately compensates risk.

Changing the revenue rule alone does not change OC. Target OC and required return cannot both be guaranteed by choosing a percentage.
