Research brief / 001

What governance reveals about collective intelligence.

A comparative study of how networked communities allocate attention, authority, participation, and incentives when no one can rely on command and control.

Anode / Governance researchPublished July 2026

The question

More voices do not guarantee better decisions. Collective intelligence depends on whether a system knows what to ask of whom, when to ask it, and how to turn a signal into action.

Governance gives us a useful proving ground because the work is visible: proposals, objections, delegations, votes, incentives, and execution leave a trace. By studying those traces across organizations, we can see where participation creates useful information—and where it creates noise, delay, or responsibility without authority.

Study scope

The research record includes 59K forum posts analyzed, 15 DAOs in one comparative study, and 4.8K governance threads mapped. We look across the full decision cycle rather than treating a vote as the whole governance event.

What we map

  • Who raises an issue, who frames it, and who has the context to evaluate it.
  • How proposals move from discussion to decision, and where they stall.
  • Which incentives shape attention, delegation, dissent, and follow-through.
  • How formal authority differs from practical influence.
  • What happens after a decision: implementation, feedback, revision, or quiet abandonment.

Why it matters beyond DAOs

The underlying design problems are not crypto-specific. Any networked organization must decide how to combine local knowledge with shared direction, how to reward work that is hard to observe, and how to preserve a meaningful voice without requiring everyone to decide everything.

This brief is the research foundation for Anode’s work on decision systems, incentive design, and coordination architecture. Contact Anode to discuss the methods or apply the questions to your organization.
